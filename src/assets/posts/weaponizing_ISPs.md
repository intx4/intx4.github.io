# Weaponizing ISPs
We can all agree on online censorship by governments being a terrible idea. Now imagine if such tremendous power was given to some random private company. That would be even worst, right? Well, welcome to Italy, 2024!

## AGCOM Piracy Shield 🛡️
On July 14, 2023, AGCOM (the Italian Agency that oversees on online communications) deliberated the creation of an online platform, the Piracy Shield, aiming to contrast online piracy. The idea is dead simple: private companies holding owner rights on digital content (read, streaming of online football - soccer - matches, a very big thing in Italy) can open a ticket to the platform, specifying an IPv4/IPv6 address or a FQDN (fully qualified domain name) guilty of distributing pirated content, and a "proof" of copyright violation. ISPs in Italy are required to build integration with platform, and act in due diligence by replying to the above mentioned tickets within 30 minutes, blocking access to the target. What could go wrong?

## Why the Piracy Shield is an awful idea💡
There are a few (many) reasons why the Piracy Shield is not only a bad idea that cannot work technically, but is also a bad, non working idea, very badly implemented.
First off, blocking resources by DNS or IP address is extremely hard to effectively implement in practice. Say I open a ticket against 'pirate.com'. Major ISPs in Italy will either remove the DNS record for 'pirate.com' from their DNS servers, or point the domain to a stop page. How can I bypass this? Piece of cake: I just change the DNS server I am reaching out to another one, like Google or OpenDNS. It is more tricky to bypass the IP block, but a commercial VPN will very easily do the trick. So Piracy Shield already failed its purpose, very nice.

Now, an even more serious point. With this technology, Italy is *de-facto' weaponizing its ISPs and making them a censorship machine, and worst than that, it is giving the front door's key to private companies (namely, Lega Serie A, Sky, Mediaset and DAZN, that make up the Italian football TV lobby, which, by the way, are also the ones that developed the platform in the first place, and "donated" it to AGCOM). 
Technically speaking, it is not clear how the "proof of piracy" should work, and it's not transparent (at all) how the proof is verified (or if it actually is). Furthermore, the operational constraints of the platform are non-sensical:
- Companies opening a ticket have 60s (!) to make any correction to the ticket
- ISPs have 30m to block the target
- Any revocation can be done only after 6 months (by the law itself)
These have led ISPs to implement an automated pipeline to block targets flagged by tickets on the Piracy Shield. As a result, private companies have a way to censor basically any online resource without restrictions. Sure, there is a caveat: the Piracy Shield comes with a whitelist maintained by AGCOM, which should contain all nation critical services to avoid major DoS attacks on our own infra. Spoiler alert: it does not.

## A carousel of incidents 🎪
The concerns of all most prominent experts on the Italian IT panorama (shared also by less tech savvy people, but surprisingly not by AGCOM or the Piracy Shield creators), manifested in a series of embarrassing incidents. The most prominent one happened actually yesterday (October 24, 2024), when a ticket blocked one IP belonging to Google CDN in Milan, where 70% of Google traffic in Italy goes through. Funnily enough, this impacted also DAZN, one of the football streamer with access to the Piracy Shield. Also, from a look on X today, it seems that even the stop page belonging to Tiscali (one of the biggest italian ISP), was blocked. This incident effectively shows two things:
- how error prone the ticket process is, how dangerous having a completely insecure and automated pipeline for blocking is, and how faulty the whitelist mechanism is.
- it is only a matter of time before the Piracy Shield backfires at the Italian government and AGCOM, possibly bringing down some critical service (think about an identity provider for SPID, the italian digital identity system used to access basically all government services, including healthcare or wealfare)
